Microsoft Defender for Cloud · Pre-Purchase Plan

Commit once, and every Defender for Cloud plan gets cheaper.

The Pre-Purchase Plan lets you buy Defender Commit Units up front and draw them down against your Defender for Cloud consumption for twelve months. The discount is published, tiered and automatic — the more units you buy, the deeper it goes.

Put simply

It works like buying gift cards for a restaurant you eat at every week. You hand over $82 and get $100 of credit — and every meal draws down that same balance, whether it is the appetiser, the main or the dessert. Each dish still costs menu price; you simply bought the dollars for less than a dollar. The caveats are the same as a gift card too: the balance is good for twelve months, and you only come out ahead if you actually eat through it.

Built-in volume discount
10 – 22%

The discount is programmatic

Ten published tiers, from 5,000 to 350,000 units. The rate is set by the tier you buy — nothing to negotiate and nothing to approve. Buying more units moves you down the ladder automatically.

Term
12 months

Use the balance whenever you like

Unlike VM reservations, commit units do not expire hourly. They sit in a pool and are drawn down as you consume, at any point across the term — so seasonal or uneven usage is not penalised.

Coverage
One balance

It covers all of Defender for Cloud

There is no ratio and no per-plan allocation. Servers, CSPM, Containers, SQL, Storage, App Service, Key Vault, APIs and AI all deduct from the same pool — which is what makes Defender for Cloud spend simpler to manage.

How the rate is set. Microsoft does not convert at live market spot. Non-USD prices are calculated in US dollars and converted using London closing spot rates captured two business days before the last business day of the previous month. That single rate then applies to every transaction for the whole of the following month, which is why the figure on the pricing page only moves once a month. The page pulls the current rate live from Azure on load; if that is blocked, the built-in fallback (1.38605, Azure's published September 2026 rate) is used, and it reproduces every Canadian price on the pricing page exactly.

Section 03 — How the Pre-Purchase Plan works

How Defender commit tiers work

A commit unit is worth one US dollar of list-price Defender for Cloud consumption. There is no conversion ratio to work out: forecast twelve months of spend at retail, and that number is how many units you need. Usage deducts from the pool automatically — nothing to redeploy, nothing to assign.

Section 06 — Making the purchase

How it is actually bought

Four practical points

A Pre-Purchase Plan is bought as an Azure reservation, not as a licence or an amendment. The mechanics below come from the Microsoft Learn documentation and determine who can buy it and what it covers.

1
Bought from the Azure portal reservations page

In the Azure portal go to Reservations → + Add and select Microsoft Defender for Cloud Pre-Purchase Plan. Choose the subscription that pays, choose the scope, then choose how many commit units to buy and complete the purchase. Current purchase steps on Microsoft Learn ↗

2
You need the right role

Owner or Reservation purchaser on an Azure subscription. On Enterprise Agreements the Reserved Instances enrollment policy must be enabled, or you must be an EA admin.

3
Scope decides what it covers

Choose resource group, single subscription, management group, or shared — which applies the balance across every eligible subscription in the billing context. Shared scope is the usual choice.

4
Charges can draw on existing Azure prepayment

The up-front cost is deducted from the enrollment's Azure Prepayment balance where one exists, or billed as overage — so an existing MACC can fund the purchase.

Utilisation is visible after purchase under Reservations in the Azure portal, so draw-down against the balance can be tracked through the term rather than discovered at renewal.
